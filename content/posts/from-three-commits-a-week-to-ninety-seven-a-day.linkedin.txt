For most of 2026 I made about 3 commits a week. In 24 of 38 weeks I made none.

Since October my repositories have seen 1,441 commits. The busiest day was 97 commits across 11 repositories. 88 of those were not mine.

They came from the team of AI agents I run on one server. Engineers write the change, a separate reviewer agent gates it, and nothing reaches main without a clean review. Merges into main went from 7 in nine months to 389 in two.

What that volume taught me:

1. Commits are exhaust. Measure what ships and gets verified live, not the commit count.
2. If agents commit as you, you need attribution. GitHub credited me with all of it until I tagged every commit by who actually made it.
3. A review loop needs an exit. Cap the fix rounds and send minor leftovers to a backlog, or the loop never ends.
4. Many writers means stale copies. Pull first, always.
5. Volume multiplies small bugs, and also makes them visible enough to find.

I write less code by hand than I did in September, and far more of my code reaches production. My job moved to deciding what matters, writing the rules and reviewing the hard calls.
